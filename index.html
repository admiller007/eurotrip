Twelve Days Eastward

One long push east from Chicago and then a slow walk back down the map: four nights in Paris, a hop over the Alps to Florence, a fast train to Rome for Shabbat in the Ghetto, and a 5:30 a.m. flight to Tel Aviv. Here is the whole route, stop by stop, with the tickets that got me there.

    The overnight from O'Hare lands at Charles de Gaulle just after nine in the morning, which is the cruelest and best way to arrive in Paris — you get a whole extra day, you just have to stay upright for it. Terminal 1, a train into the city, keys to an apartment between the Pompidou and Bastille, and then straight into it.

    Two full days, run hard. The Louvre in a small group with reserved access, which is the only sane way to see the Mona Lisa in August. Then out to Versailles the next morning on an electric bike — the gardens are enormous and the bike is the difference between seeing the palace and seeing the estate, hameau and all. Dinners both nights, and one very Parisian evening at the Crazy Horse to close it out.

    Out of Orly rather than Charles de Gaulle, over the Alps, and down into Florence before lunch. Florence airport is close enough to the centre that you can be standing in front of a Renaissance facade an hour after landing, which is exactly what happened.

    The city runs on timed entry now, so the day was built around a 13:30 slot: a 72-hour combined ticket for the Accademia and the Bargello. David in the afternoon, then Donatello and the sculpture courts.

    And then, at five, out of the city entirely — e-bikes up into the hills, which is where you finally understand the shape of the place. Florence sits in a bowl, and from above at golden hour the whole Renaissance skyline is one object: dome, tower, river, hills. Back down for dinner, a free day after that, and an early start on the third morning.

    Two hours and two minutes, Florence to Rome, at nine in the morning. The Italo is the good way to do this — you leave one Renaissance city after breakfast and you're in an imperial one before noon, and the Tuscan countryside goes by the window at 300 kilometres an hour.

    It was a Friday, which set the shape of the whole stay. Into Termini before lunch, and then straight onto the back of a Vespa for an hour and three quarters through the city with a photographer riding along — the fastest possible introduction to Rome, and the only way to cover that much of it before Shabbat. Keys by five — to an apartment a few streets off the Portico d'Ottavia — and then dinner that night in the Jewish Ghetto — a neighbourhood that has been doing exactly this in exactly this place for two thousand years. Rome for the weekend, and then the earliest alarm of the trip.

    A 05:30 departure out of Fiumicino Terminal 3, seat 31A, wheels down at Ben Gurion at ten in the morning. Four and a half hours from the Roman Ghetto to Tel Aviv, which is a strange and good thing to think about at that altitude — the same story, the two ends of it, one morning apart.

    Three days at the far end of the map, and the real reason the whole route bent east in the first place. Everything before this was the long way round.

Depart Zurich at one in the afternoon, land at O'Hare Terminal 5 at four — the one flight on the trip that gives the day back instead of taking it.

    Twelve days, four countries, five cities, and one ticket book's worth of proof that it happened.
